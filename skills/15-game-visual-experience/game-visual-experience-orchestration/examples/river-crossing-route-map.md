# Worked example: river-crossing visual-experience route

Evidence level: documented example; no build, device, child, learning or cultural result is claimed.

| Loop step | Player question and presentation | Owner and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Approach | “Which crossing is safe?” Water speed, debris and guide gesture lead; a compact objective appears only after hesitation. | Art direction + game UI; prototype observation required |
| Inspect | High-contrast current markers, controller/touch focus, audio and haptic alternatives reveal timing. | Game feel + access; device and affected-player checks required |
| Commit | Input feedback confirms the chosen route before movement; camera preserves landing visibility. | Game feel; build-executed and device-measured evidence required |
| Recover | Failure returns to the bank with a plain cause and one changed clue, never a purchase or ad rescue. | UI + children/learning; child playtest required |
| Complete | The child explains the observed river cue; celebration is brief and skippable. | Children/learning; learning measure is separate from enjoyment |

An ad is allowed only after the completion state and return-to-map choice is clear, never before retry, save, stopping or exit. For a child audience, the placement remains blocked until store, privacy, guardian and accountable legal review permit it. No-fill returns directly to the map.

Route result: conditional design specification. Cultural river knowledge, learning transfer, performance, accessibility and ad trust are `not assessed` until their named evidence exists.
