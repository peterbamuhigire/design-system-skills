# Worked example: river-cue learning loop

Evidence level: documented example only; no educational effectiveness or child-safety approval is claimed.

Objective: after guided and varied crossings, the learner identifies one visible cue of stronger current and chooses the safer of two routes. Transfer outside the game is explicitly not claimed.

| Stage | Experience | Evidence question |
|---|---|---|
| Model | A guide points to debris speed while the child controls the camera | Can the child attend to the cue without the guide naming the answer? |
| Guided practice | Two routes differ strongly; an optional hint repeats the cue | Is the choice understood or guessed? |
| Variation | Lighting and route shape change while the current cue persists | Does recognition survive irrelevant visual change? |
| Independent attempt | No pointer appears; feedback explains the observed cue after choice | Can the child choose and explain without prompting? |
| Stop | Save-and-exit is always visible; completion gives a calm stopping point | Can the child stop without loss, nagging or accidental continuation? |

Enjoyment, completion and time played do not prove learning. Ads remain disabled until the age/territory/store/privacy/legal/guardian gate passes; even then, no placement is allowed inside this loop or before retry, explanation, save or exit.
