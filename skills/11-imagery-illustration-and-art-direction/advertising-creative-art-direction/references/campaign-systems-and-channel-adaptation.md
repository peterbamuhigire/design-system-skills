# Campaign Systems and Channel Adaptation

Parent skill: [`../SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md) (`advertising-creative-art-direction`).

**When to read:** when one idea must become many units across channels and weeks, and when
building placement layouts. Sources: Landa (2022) *Strategic Creativity*, Routledge (campaign
structure, lodestar idea, press-release test attributed there to Rob Reilly of WPP, channel
tailoring); Adams et al. (2012) *Graphic Design Rules* (thumbnail and above-the-fold tests).

---

## 1. Triplets and cousins

| System | Rules | Strength | Risk | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Triplets** | One master template: same type alignments, element positions, palette, typefaces and image style; only the image and headline change | Instant recognition; impressions add up | Later units can bore people who saw the first | Outdoor, transit and taxi branding, prospecting social, point of sale, display |
| **Cousins** | Family resemblance: shared assets, varied composition, palette emphasis and visualisation | Renews interest on each exposure | Drift into unrelated units | Long-running organic social, editorial content, brand campaigns |

In both systems lock the **distinctive brand assets** - logo, colour, typeface, tone, sound, any
character - as the red thread, and keep **one imagery treatment** across all units (if one unit is
black-and-white with hard light, all are; if one is illustrated, all are).

## 2. Big-idea test pack (run before layouts)

The marketing engine owns the big idea and its wording. This engine tests whether it can be
executed visually:

1. **One sentence.** Write the campaign idea as one sentence: what people should think or feel
   plus the mechanism. If the team cannot agree the sentence, stop.
2. **Three-channel sketch.** Sketch the idea in at least three different channels (for example
   outdoor or print, short video, social or creator content, point of sale or event). If it only
   works in one, it is an execution, not a campaign idea.
3. **Press-release test.** Write the short news story the market would tell about the idea once
   it lands. If it cannot be written honestly, the idea is not strong enough.
4. **Forward test (East Africa).** Would the idea survive being forwarded on WhatsApp as one image
   plus two lines? If not, simplify the key visual.
5. **Unit tag.** Tag each unit "brand building" or "transaction" (or both) so layout priorities
   are clear: brand units lead with the idea and assets; transaction units lead with offer,
   price and action.

## 3. Integrated campaign checklist

- Every unit ladders to the one idea.
- Each unit is conceived for its channel and gives the audience something worth their time
  (information, utility, entertainment, social good or temptation), not the brand talking
  about itself.
- The art direction distinguishes the brand from category competitors.
- One simple message across channels, executed natively per channel: longer video carries story;
  print, outdoor and social need a quicker single message.
- People avoid advertising; the unit must earn attention in the first glance.
- For long-running brands, document the campaign "world": visual style, characters and
  behaviour rules that govern all future executions.

## 4. Channel tailoring notes

| Channel | Visual priorities |
|---|---|
| Outdoor, billboards, taxi and bus branding | Very few words; one image; brand identifiable at distance and speed; test legibility at the real viewing distance |
| Paid and organic social feed | First-frame clarity at phone size; captions for sound-off video; respect text-free zones |
| Stories and short vertical video | Key message and brand inside the safe zone; hook in the first seconds; subtitles |
| Print press and magazines | Publication's current advertisement specification (trim, bleed, ink limit, file format); see `print-production-and-finishing` |
| Point of sale and packaging | Shelf distance and angle; thumbnail test for online listings |
| Creator or influencer content | Brand guardrails, not templates; see section 6 |
| Radio plus visual | Visual units echo the audio signature and line |

## 5. Placement specification check

Specifications change. Always build to the platform's or publisher's **current** specification,
record the source and the date checked, and re-check at each campaign.

Verified example (currentness register claim AD-08, source: Meta Ads Guide, checked
2026-09-23, review due 2027-03-23):
- Facebook and Instagram feed images: recommended 4:5 at 1440 × 1800; supported range 1.91:1 to
  4:5.
- Stories and Reels: 9:16 at 1440 × 2560.
- Keep roughly the top 14 per cent, bottom 35 per cent and 6 per cent at each side free of text,
  logos and key elements.
- The older 1:1 at 1080 × 1080 default is outdated for these placements.

For every other platform (TikTok, LinkedIn, X, YouTube, Google display, newspapers, outdoor
contractors), treat sizes and safe zones as a check against that owner's current published
specification. Do not rely on remembered numbers. If the specification cannot be checked, mark
the placement `NOT_ASSESSED` and block release.

Record per placement:

| Placement | Aspect and size | Safe zones | File type and weight limit | Captions and alt text | Source and date checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 6. Creator-supplied and user-generated content

- Give creators brand guardrails (assets, colours to avoid, disclosure placement, prohibited
  claims), not rigid templates.
- Acceptance criteria for delivered assets: resolution adequate for the placement, clean lighting,
  consistent editing within the campaign treatment, legible text at thumbnail size, correct
  disclosure as required by the marketing engine's legal gate.
- Rights: record usage rights, duration and territories before reuse in paid media.
