# Creative Effectiveness Screen and Critique Protocol

Parent skill: [`../SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md) (`advertising-creative-art-direction`).

**When to read:** before any concept reaches the client, when presenting concepts, and whenever
client or internal feedback needs translating into direction.

Sources: Landa (2022) *Strategic Creativity*, Routledge (effectiveness scale, critique guide,
responsible-creative interrogation, presentation and review practices reported from senior
practitioners); Kelley and Sheehan (c. 2021) *Advertising Management in a Digital Environment*,
Routledge (creative assessment rules, brief as yardstick). The scale adapts ladders that agencies
use internally; it is an internal screen, not an industry standard. Scores are judgement, not
measurement of market effect.

---

## 1. The 7-point effectiveness scale (concept screen)

| Score | Level | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Harmful | Damages people, culture or the brand; stereotype, deception, legal breach |
| 2 | Invisible | Undifferentiated; nobody will notice; money wasted |
| 3 | Off-brand | Misses the brand's purpose, voice or personality |
| 4 | Not an idea | Decent execution with nothing that builds the brand |
| 5 | On-brand, not arresting | Right, but will not stop anyone |
| 6 | Short-term attention | Gets noticed; weak long-term brand building |
| 7 | Strategically creative | Builds the brand over time, earns attention, moves people to act, gets brand and audience right |

**Procedure:**
1. The reviewer restates the brief's objective and audience before scoring.
2. Each reviewer scores each concept independently before discussion, with a one-line reason.
3. Any concept scoring 1 is killed, whatever its craft.
4. Scores of 2-4 are killed or rethought from the insight.
5. Score 5 goes back for a sharper hook.
6. Only 6 and 7 advance. Flag reviewer disagreement of more than two points and discuss the
   reason, not the average.

The marketing engine's creative review gate remains the release gate for finished assets. This
scale screens ideas earlier; a high score here never waives evidence, rights or safety checks.

## 2. Concept screen checklist (with the scale)

- Poster test and cover tests pass (`copy-image-constructions.md`).
- One focal point, one takeaway, one call to action.
- The idea gives the audience a benefit, not the brand talking about itself.
- It fits the brand's long-term story and distinctive assets.
- The likely negative reactions are listed for client sign-off before launch.

## 3. Responsible-creative visual checks

1. **Swap test:** swap the depicted group for another. Does a stereotype appear?
2. **Punch up, not down:** humour aimed at the powerful is satire; humour at the vulnerable is
   harm.
3. **Power and gaze:** who holds power in the image, who looks at whom, who is active?
4. **Appropriation:** whose story or symbol is this, and is it ours to use? Is sacred or cultural
   material respected and credited?
5. **East African trope list:** helpless-beneficiary imagery, ethnic humour, stereotyped rural
   people, house helps or boda-boda riders, skin-lightening aesthetics, religious insensitivity,
   narrow gender roles in household advertising, political party colours or symbols during
   election periods.
6. **Disability:** avoid the "inspiration", infantilising and pity tropes; show disabled people as
   ordinary participants.
7. Route legal questions (claims, regulated categories, data, content restrictions) to the
   marketing engine's legal and market release gate. Do not decide them here.

## 4. Presenting creative

1. Spend the first part re-framing the assignment: the task, the approach, who was consulted,
   what was learned, what was tried.
2. Show the concepts with conviction. Never open with "So, what do you think?"
3. Close with next steps: review, select a direction, agree timing.
4. Offer the client time: do not force a decision in the room. A short follow-up session (about
   an hour) to discuss what each idea is really doing, and its likely negative reactions,
   produces better decisions than instant votes.
5. Ask for one named decision-maker at the briefing and at every key review; avoid decisions
   carried "upstairs" by a messenger or consensus by circulation.

## 5. Critique rules for reviewers

- Judge against the brief and the agreed criteria, not personal taste.
- State the problem, not the solution: say what is not yet working, not how to fix it.
- Say what works as well as what does not, and why.
- Use design and idea vocabulary: focal point, hierarchy, contrast, palette, insight, idea,
  brand voice, call to action.
- Check where your taste comes from: familiarity, personal liking, or fit with brand and audience?
- Block inappropriate feedback: dictation without reasons, notes from people who have not read the
  brief, feedback that changes the brief mid-stream, and notes that reduce designers to
  production operators.
- Test to validate or reduce risk, not to decide; research tends to favour the familiar.

## 6. Feedback translations

| Heard | Translate to |
|---|---|
| "I don't like the green." | "The green reads close to [competitor]. Can we find a colour that is unmistakably ours?" |
| "Make the logo bigger." | "Brand linkage looks weak; can the brand appear earlier or more clearly in the first glance?" |
| "It's boring." | "Which brief goal is not yet met: attention, clarity or distinctiveness?" |
| "Make it like [competitor]'s ad." | "What are you responding to in it: warmth, confidence, simplicity? Let us bring that quality to your brand in our own way." |
| "My colleague's friend thinks…" | Do not relay. Filter to feedback grounded in the brief. |
| "Can we add more information?" | "Which single message must survive? The rest can move to the landing page or caption." |
