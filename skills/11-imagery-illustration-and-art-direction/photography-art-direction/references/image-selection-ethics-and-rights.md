# Image Selection, Ethics and Rights

Parent skill: [`../SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md) (`photography-art-direction`).

**When to read:** when choosing between candidate images, cropping other people's photographs,
handling client-supplied images, depicting people (especially beneficiaries, children and
patients), and before publication. Sources: Adams, Dawson, Foster and Seddon (2012, revised
edition) *Graphic Design Rules*, Frances Lincoln (Imagery section); Landa (2022) *Strategic
Creativity*, Routledge (power, gaze and representation); McNeil (2013) *The Web Designer's Idea
Book, Volume 3*, HOW Books (non-profit imagery tone).

---

## 1. Selection rules

- **Content over technical perfection:** pick the image with the right content and emotion over a
  technically perfect weaker one.
- **Support, do not repeat:** the image adds to the text; the caption adds information rather than
  describing the obvious.
- **The unexpected pairing is remembered:** an image with a word that the viewer must complete
  is stronger than a literal illustration of the headline.
- **Stock is not a saving if competitors can download the same frame.** Check specialist
  libraries before commissioning an impossible shot; commission originals when budget allows.
- **Phones are not always enough** for large print; check resolution at the final size.

## 2. People and power

- Every group photograph carries political content: viewers read age, gender, ethnicity, class,
  disability and power. Decide deliberately who is shown, doing what.
- **Face-ism:** face-dominant framing tends to read as intellect and authority; body-dominant
  framing as physicality. Historically men were framed face-first and women body-first. Crop
  consistently across people.
- **Dignity first:** show people served by NGOs, clinics and schools as agents with names and
  roles, not as passive recipients. No "poverty" imagery. Put the reader's action at the centre.
- **Consent and safeguarding:** written consent for identifiable people; the organisation's
  child-safeguarding and data-protection policy governs images of children and patients. Verify
  current legal obligations with the client's legal owner.
- **Shock imagery** only when briefed and signed off with its likely reactions.

## 3. East African sourcing

- Generic "African" stock is often from another region and shows it. Invest in local shoots, even
  modest ones on a proper camera; authenticity is visible to local audiences.
- Brief photographers on local settings, dress, vehicles and signage so the frames are credible.
- For low-literacy or multilingual audiences, prefer literal images and icons over abstract
  symbols, and test comprehension with users.

## 4. Handling other people's images

- **Client-supplied images** come pre-approved; make them the best they can be. Where one fails,
  show a better alternative side by side with the reason.
- **Do not over-crop** a well-composed photograph, and do not re-crop landscape to portrait (or the
  reverse); ask for the alternative orientation.
- **Gutters:** never run a face or fine detail across a binding gutter or fold.
- **No disguise:** do not hide a weak image with filters, fake sepia or heavy desaturation; get a
  better image, reshoot, draw it, or make the rough quality an explicit idea.
- **Silhouettes and drop shadows** sparingly; overuse looks like a catalogue.
- **Credit and respect creators:** credit photographers and illustrators; never edit an
  illustrator's work without permission; contracts often forbid it.

Keep an "approved and current" folder separate from archive; withdraw expired assets from all
live channels.

## 6. Asset hygiene

- Keep layered, high-bit-depth masters; export production copies per surface.
- Name files with stable codes, not page numbers that change; add keywords in metadata.
- Colour-correct in order (tonal balance, curves, cast), sharpen last and lightly for the output.
