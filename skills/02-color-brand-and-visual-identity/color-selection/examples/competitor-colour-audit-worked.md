# Worked example: competitor colour audit and rationale

Fictional client used to show the method: "Kyamula Microfinance", a Kampala lender to market
traders. Competitor names are replaced by descriptions; verify real brands' current colours
before any client use.

## Client colour brief (answers recorded)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Must keep | Nothing; new brand |
| Avoid | Red (board associates it with debt collection notices); any party colour |
| Markets | Kampala, Wakiso, Mukono; later Mbarara |
| Audience | Traders 25-55, mostly on mid-range Android phones |
| Longevity | Ten years or more |
| Media | Signage and printed forms (digital print) plus app and WhatsApp |
| Spot budget | None; CMYK builds only |

## Competitor map (descriptive)

| Competitor type | Primary | Accent | Lightness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large commercial bank A | Navy | Light blue | Dark |
| Large commercial bank B | Royal blue | White | Mid-dark |
| Microfinance deposit-taker C | Forest green | Yellow | Dark |
| SACCO network D | Green | Orange | Mid |
| Mobile-money leader | Bright yellow | Black | Light |

Category convention: blue and green, dark. Bright yellow is owned by a mobile-money leader.

## Decisions

1. **Stand apart** from blue and green; avoid yellow (owned) and red (client exclusion).
2. **Temperature:** warm, to feel approachable next to cold bank blues.
3. **Lightness:** deep primary for signage legibility; light warm neutral backgrounds for forms.
4. **Hue source:** sampled from a photograph of dried millet and clay pots in a Kampala market:
   deep terracotta primary, millet-gold accent, warm off-white ground, charcoal text.
5. **Greyscale test:** terracotta and gold separated by lightness; pass.
6. **Culture check:** five traders and two staff asked what the colours suggest; answers noted
   ("market", "pots", "warm"); no negative associations recorded in this small check, which is
   not a survey.

## Rationale lines (for the brand guide)

- "Our terracotta was chosen to stand apart from a category dominated by navy, blue and green. It
  is warm to signal approachability, and it was checked with traders in Kampala markets, where it
  read as familiar and local."
- "Millet gold is our action colour, used only for buttons and key figures."
