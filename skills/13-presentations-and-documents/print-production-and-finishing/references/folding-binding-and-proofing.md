# Folding, Binding and Proofing

Parent skill: [`../SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md) (`print-production-and-finishing`).

**When to read:** when setting up the geometry of a folded, bound or dimensional job, and when
planning proofs, press checks and the schedule. Figures are working defaults recorded by Adams,
Dawson, Foster and Seddon (*Graphic Design Rules*, Frances Lincoln, 2012, revised edition);
the printer's and binder's figures govern.

---

## 1. Page sizes

- ISO 216 A-series for most East African and international work (A4 = 210 × 297 mm). C-series
  envelopes match (a C4 envelope takes A4 flat; C5 takes A4 folded once). RA and SRA sizes are
  the untrimmed press sheets. US clients use Letter-based sizes; confirm before building.
- Check the press sheet size against the imposition to control paper waste; paper is often the
  largest cost in a job.

## 2. Bleed, safety and trim

| Item | Working default | Confirm with |
|---|---|---|
| Bleed | At least 3 mm on every edge element; some printers ask for 5 mm | Printer |
| Safety for small items (folios, small type) | About 5-6 mm inside trim | Printer |
| Safety for text blocks and key images | Wider, often about twice the small-item figure | Printer and binder |
| Spine | No bleed across the spine unless the binder asks for it | Binder |
| Thick books | Allow for gutter "bunching" where pages curve into the binding | Binder |

## 3. Folds

- Panels that fold in are made slightly narrower (commonly about 2 mm for a roll fold on
  standard weights) so the leaflet closes flat. Get exact panel widths from the printer for the
  stock weight.
- Heavy stock needs creasing (scoring) before folding to stop cracking, especially across the
  grain and on dark solids.
- Design folded pieces as the reader meets them: front panel, first inside view, full spread.
  Test with a paper dummy.

## 4. Binding choice

| Method | Suits | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Saddle stitch (staples) | Booklets, programmes, short reports | Page limit depends on stock (the source records about 96 pages on normal weights; the binder's figure governs); creep |
| Perfect binding (glued spine) | Reports and books with enough bulk for a spine | Gutter loss of several millimetres; lay-flat is poor |
| PUR binding | Reports that must open flatter and survive heat | Cost; local availability |
| Wire-O or spiral | Manuals, workbooks, training materials that must lie flat | Hole margin; cover wrap options |
| Case binding (hardback) | Premium books, commemorative reports | Cost and lead time; needs bulk |

## 5. Creep and gutter loss

- **Creep:** in saddle-stitched jobs, inner sheets push outward, so after trimming the inner
  pages lose outer margin. The printer's imposition applies a creep allowance; keep live content
  well inside the outer safety on centre pages.
- **Gutter loss:** perfect binding swallows some of the inner margin (the book records up to
  about 5 mm). Widen inner margins and keep text and faces out of the gutter.
- Never run a face or a detailed image across the gutter; watch crossover type on spreads.

## 6. Dummies and mock-ups

- Order a **paper dummy** on the specified stock for any bound or folded job and show it to the
  client. It prevents "too flimsy" surprises on delivery.
- Build a **full-size mock-up** for packaging and dimensional work: test folds, reading angle on
  the shelf, legal copy position, and fit in the shipping case.

## 7. Proofs

| Proof | Use | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Soft proof (monitor, colour-managed) | Early layout and content checks | Not colour-accurate; only when time outranks quality |
| Digital contract proof | Colour approval on a calibrated device to the press profile | Stock may differ from the job stock |
| Proof on the actual stock (or wet proof) | Brand colour, covers, packaging | Cost and time |
| Office laser or inkjet print | Content check only | **Never** a colour proof |

- Get a hard proof for every job, including single-colour work.
- The client approves the proof, not the screen. File the signed proof.

## 8. Press checks

- Attend for brand-critical colour, covers, packaging and large runs; even small jobs benefit.
- Bring the signed proof and the specification sheet. Check colour against the proof under the
  agreed light, registration, ink density across the sheet, hickeys and marks, and trim and fold
  positions on a folded sample.
- Do not pretend to know more than the press operator, and do not accept "impossible" without an
  explanation. Record every adjustment and the approved sheet.

## 9. Schedule and handover

- Build contingency days into every print schedule; pass schedule changes to the printer
  immediately.
- Confirm client-supplied materials (logos, photographs, copy) meet specification on receipt,
  not at deadline.
- For advertisements in newspapers and magazines, read each publication's current advertisement
  specification (trim, bleed, ink limit, screen, file format) before designing.
- Back up and archive the final files, proof and specification off-site.
