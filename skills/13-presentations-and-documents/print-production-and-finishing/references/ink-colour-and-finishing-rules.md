# Ink, Colour and Finishing Rules

Parent skill: [`../SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md) (`print-production-and-finishing`).

**When to read:** when building colours, blacks, tints and effects for a printed job, and when
choosing finishes. The numbers are practitioner starting points recorded by Adams, Dawson,
Foster and Seddon (*Graphic Design Rules*, Frances Lincoln, 2012, revised edition). They are
**not** specifications. Each press, stock and profile differs; the printer's current figure
always governs. Industry standards for offset print conditions sit in the ISO 12647 series; ask
the printer which condition and profile they print to.

---

## 1. Building colour for print

| Rule | Why | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Specify from a physical process or spot colour guide, never from screen | Screens are backlit and use a different gamut | Current guide, not a faded one; show the client the guide |
| Never specify print colour in RGB | RGB gamut is larger; conversions shift colour | All brand swatches have print builds |
| Limit special inks | Each spot adds a plate and cost; common practice is at most one or two specials plus varnish alongside CMYK | Printer confirms press unit count |
| Paper whiteness changes apparent colour | Blue-white stock makes colours look brighter; cream stock warms them | Proof on the chosen stock |
| Colours shift under different light and surroundings | Complex colours (violets, greys) move more than primaries | Warn the client; view proofs under agreed light |

## 2. Blacks, tints and ink limits

- **Total ink (total area coverage):** a commonly cited safe ceiling is around 300 per cent;
  printers often allow somewhat more on coated stock and noticeably less on uncoated or newsprint.
  Get the figure for this press and stock. Check dark photographs (night skies, dark skin tones in
  shadow, forest scenes) where coverage climbs.
- **Rich black:** use for large solid areas only. Recipes differ by printer (for example one
  printer may ask for 50/50/50/100, another for 60/40/40/100, with warm or cool variants). Ask for
  the printer's recipe. Keep small text and fine rules in 100 per cent black only, so they do not
  mis-register.
- **Minimum tint:** very light screens (below about 7 per cent, higher on uncoated or newsprint)
  can vanish on press. Use a lighter spot colour at 100 per cent instead, or drop the tint.
- **No tints of tints:** resolve every colour to real process or spot values.
- **Coloured small type:** build with at least one process plate at 100 per cent so edges stay
  sharp.
- **Small body text** never in pure cyan, magenta or yellow.

## 3. Overprint and knock-out

- **Black text overprints** coloured backgrounds, so slight mis-registration does not leave a
  white halo. It also lets the black plate be swapped for another language edition.
- **Spot and metallic inks:** decide overprint or knock-out with the printer; metallics often need
  a knock-out and sometimes an underlay.
- **Check overprint preview** before export; accidental white overprint makes objects disappear.

## 4. Dot gain and press behaviour

- Ink spreads into paper, so midtones print darker than on screen. Gain is larger on uncoated
  stock and newsprint than on coated stock. The press profile compensates when it matches the
  real press and stock; ask which profile the printer uses.
- Budget printers can run uncoated jobs noticeably dark. Choose printers who measure and control
  gain, and hold them to the signed proof.
- **Packaging long runs:** some practitioners prepare files slightly lighter than the approved
  proof because long packaging runs tend to darken on press. Only do this on the printer's
  advice for that press.

## 5. Images for print

- Effective resolution about 300 ppi at placed size for litho (about twice the screen ruling).
  Large-format and digital devices have different needs; follow the printer.
- Do not enlarge placed images much beyond 100 per cent; replace or rescale at source.
- Keep layered, high-bit-depth masters for editing; place flattened production copies.
- Colour-correct in order: tonal balance (levels), then curves, then colour casts; sharpen last
  and lightly for the output size.
- Do not rescue a weak image with filters, fake sepia or heavy desaturation. Get a better
  image, reshoot, draw it, or make the low quality an explicit, large, deliberate choice.

## 6. Finishes

| Finish | Use | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Gloss lamination | Durability, colour saturation, food and handling | Glare; can read as cheap if overused. "Gloss equals premium" is outdated |
| Matt or soft-touch lamination | Contemporary, tactile covers | Scuff marks on dark matt covers; test |
| Spot varnish | Emphasis on a logo or image | Registration tolerance; keep away from fine type |
| Foil (hot or cold) | Premium accents, packaging, certificates | Minimum line weights; test on stock; local availability |
| Embossing (raised) / debossing (pressed in) | Tactile identity, stationery, covers | Stock weight and grain; reverse side shows the impression |
| Die-cut and unusual folds | Pieces people keep | Die cost and set-up; converter's template governs |
| Endpapers, head and tail bands | Hardback delight at modest cost | Binding method must allow them |

Choose one finish that serves the idea. Weigh effect against cost; avoid "kitchen-sink"
finishing. Test-proof every special finish in advance.

## 7. Screen print and signage

- Screen-print artwork must be vector or halftone at 100 per cent colour, with no tints unless
  the printer screens them.
- Large-format signage: ask for the viewing distance; resolution needs fall as distance grows.
- Vehicle and building graphics: request the installer's template and panel breaks.
