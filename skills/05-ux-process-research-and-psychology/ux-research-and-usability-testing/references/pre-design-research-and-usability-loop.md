# Pre-Design Research and the Lightweight Usability Loop

Parent skill: [`../SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md) (`ux-research-and-usability-testing`).

**When to read:** before visual design starts on a new product or redesign, and when setting up a
small, repeatable usability loop. Paraphrased from Cao, J. et al. (2015) *Web UI Design for the
Human Eye*, UXPin (pre-design research tripod) and Krug, S. (2010) *Rocket Surgery Made Easy*,
New Riders (do-it-yourself usability testing). Absent testing stays `NOT_ASSESSED`; never invent
user evidence.

---

## 1. Pre-design research tripod

1. **Card sorting** - open sorts to learn how people group content; closed sorts to test a
   proposed structure.
2. **Behavioural interviews** - ask about what people do and have done ("Walk me through the last
   time you paid school fees"), not about preferences for micro-details ("Do you like blue
   buttons?").
3. **Competitive heuristic evaluation** - about five evaluators score each competitor against the
   same heuristics; plot the scores on overlapping radar charts. Where every competitor scores well,
   the convention is expected: do not break it without reason. Where none scores well, there is a
   design opportunity.

## 1b. Process spine and flow notation

Discovery → design → develop → evaluate, mixing qualitative and quantitative, attitudinal and
behavioural methods. Deliverable chain: persona (one to two pages, traced to research) → journey
map → user flow. In simple flowcharts
use rectangles for screens, circles for actions and diamonds for decisions (source: *2022 Guide
to UX/UI Design in 45 Minutes for Beginners*, which follows Nielsen Norman Group's process).

## 2. Lightweight usability loop

1. Write the research question and the decision it informs.
2. Recruit three to five representative people (or honest proxies) per round.
3. Write realistic task scenarios that do not name the controls.
4. Ask participants to think aloud; stay neutral; do not lead or rescue.
5. Record consent, privacy boundaries, task success, blockers, hesitations and paraphrased quotes.
6. Rate severity, pick the smallest high-impact set of fixes, change them.
7. Retest the same tasks. Report results as directional evidence, not population estimates.
8. Remote sessions still need a script, recording consent and a view of the participant's real
   interaction.

Qualitative sessions answer where and why people struggle; analytics answer how often. Use each
for its own decision.

## 3. East African practice

Test on the devices and networks people use (mid-range Android, mobile data), in the language
they use, and compensate participants appropriately (for example airtime or data) under the
client's research-ethics rules.
