# Guerrilla and Intercept Research Sessions

Parent skill: [`../SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md) (`ux-research-and-usability-testing`).

**When to read:** when a question needs quick, low-cost evidence from real target users (a value
proposition, a concept prototype, a single flow) and a formal lab study is not justified. For
full moderated protocol and severity rating use `usability-test-protocol.md`; this file covers
planning, recruitment and logistics for short sessions in public or community settings. For
longer community fieldwork, gatekeepers and non-literate instruments use
`field-research-in-low-resource-and-cross-cultural-settings.md`.

---

## 1. When guerrilla sessions are and are not adequate

| Question | Adequate? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Is the value proposition understood and wanted by the segment? | Yes, as early signal | Short concept reactions from the right people |
| Is there a gross usability failure in one flow? | Yes | Five sessions surface severe problems |
| Does the product work for specialists in their real workplace? | No | Needs contextual inquiry on site |
| Sensitive topics (health, debt, legal, children) | No | Public settings breach privacy; needs a private, consented session |
| Prevalence or statistical comparison | No | Small, convenience samples; use a survey or analytics |

## 2. Five-step setup

1. **Objective.** Write the hypothesis and the three to five questions the round must answer.
   Anything else is out of scope for this round.
2. **Script.** Two parts: screener questions that confirm the participant belongs to the segment
   (behaviour-based, for example "Have you sent money to someone upcountry in the last month?"),
   then open behavioural questions about the problem, then the prototype task. Ask about past
   behaviour, not hypothetical intentions; never pitch.
3. **Venue and roles.** Choose where the segment already is: a taxi stage, market, SACCO banking
   hall (with permission), campus, farmers' group meeting. Roles: a coordinator (greets, screens,
   manages flow and incentives), an interviewer, and a note-taker; stakeholders observe silently.
4. **Recruitment.** Advertise through channels the segment uses (community WhatsApp groups, group
   leaders, notices at the venue). Offer a modest incentive that is useful to the participant
   (airtime, a drink, a small voucher) and does not coerce.
5. **Screen and schedule.** Screen before the session; stagger arrivals with a buffer so one no-show
   or overrun does not waste the round. Record who was screened out and why; they may show who
   does not have the problem.

## 3. Session flow (10 to 30 minutes)

1. Greet, explain purpose in plain language, obtain consent (spoken consent noted, or written),
   state what will be recorded and how long it is kept.
2. Screener, then problem questions.
3. Prototype task on the participant's own type of device where possible.
4. Close: thank, give the incentive, ask one final question ("What would stop you using this?").
5. Debrief as a team within the hour: what surprised us, what matched the hypothesis, what to
   change before the next session.

| Decision | Rule | Failure caused by the wrong choice |
|---|---|---|
| Friends or colleagues as participants | Only screened members of the segment | Polite approval that predicts nothing |
| Photos or recordings in public | Only with explicit consent; avoid faces and screens of bystanders | Privacy breach and loss of community trust |
| Interviewer explains the product | Let the prototype speak; answer questions with questions | You test your pitch, not the product |
| One round treated as proof | Label as early signal; confirm with the right method | Strategic decisions on convenience samples |
| Language | Run in the participant's preferred language with a fluent interviewer | Comprehension failures blamed on the design |

## 4. Worked example (original): school-fees payment reminder, Jinja

Hypothesis: parents who pay fees in instalments by mobile money would act on an SMS reminder that
shows the balance and a payment code. Venue: two primary-school parents' meetings (head teacher's
permission obtained). Screener: "Did you pay school fees in more than one instalment last term?"
Task: on a mid-range Android phone, read a mock SMS and show how they would pay. Team: coordinator
(Lusoga and English), interviewer, note-taker. Incentive: airtime. Ten sessions. Early signal:
most parents looked for the school's name before trusting the message; the balance format with
commas was misread by some. Result recorded as early signal for redesign; not a launch decision.

## 5. Checks

- [ ] Objective and questions written before recruiting.
- [ ] Every participant passed a behaviour-based screener.
- [ ] Consent obtained and recording limits stated; no bystanders captured.
- [ ] Incentive modest and non-coercive; venue permission obtained.
- [ ] Findings labelled as early signal with sample and setting stated.
