# Enterprise UX Maturity Checklist

Parent skill: [`../SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md) (`enterprise-ux-process`).

**When to read:** when declaring the maturity level of an engagement, assembling the
activity-by-level evidence pack, or deciding whether a product may launch or be sold as premium.
Copy the tables into the project workspace and fill the evidence column.

---

## 1. Declare the level first

| Level | Name | What the organisation actually does |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No design | Design is ignored |
| 1 | Uninformed interface styling | Cosmetic work only |
| 2 | Style and colour (problem solving) | A UX approach is defined and goals are integrated |
| 3 | UX design | Flow drives behaviour; problems are solved effectively |
| 4 | Experience design (innovation) | Design-thinking process; distinctive, memorable experience |

| Decision | Rule | Consequence of the wrong choice |
|---|---|---|
| Standard premium engagement | Evidence must reach Level 3 at minimum | Premium price without premium evidence fails the first audit |
| Top tier (luxury, regulated, mission-critical) | Evidence must reach Level 4 | Critical products launched on Level 3 evidence carry untested experience risk |
| Evidence falls short of the proposal | Declare the level the evidence supports and show the gap | Aspirational claims damage credibility |

State the level in one sentence at the top of the engagement summary: "This engagement operates at
UX maturity Level [3 / 4]."

## 2. Evidence the team must produce, by gate

A tick means the activity is expected at that level. "All" means every level, including 1 and 2.

### Gate A - Frame the problem

| Activity | Required from | Evidence that satisfies the gate |
|---|---|---|
| Problem definition and business objective | L3 | Signed answer to: what is the need, why now, for whom, and how does this make the user's life easier? |
| Stakeholder discussions or interviews | L3 | Interview notes or transcripts plus a summary brief |
| Success criteria | L3 | Signed checklist of measurable success conditions |

### Gate B - Understand users and market

| Activity | Required from | Evidence that satisfies the gate |
|---|---|---|
| User research | L3 | Qualitative and quantitative report with methods disclosed |
| User interviews | L3 | Findings on process, device use, preferences, industry exposure and context of use |
| Competitor analysis | L3 | Completed competitor profile matrix (at least five direct and three indirect) and one-page brief; see `strategy-and-collaboration-inputs.md` section 2 |
| Personas | L3 | One research-traced persona per primary user role, with the minimum fields set by the parent skill |
| User journeys | L3 | Chronological touchpoint sequence per primary persona |
| Experience maps | L4 | Visual flow with feelings, frustrations and expectations |

### Gate C - Structure the product

| Activity | Required from | Evidence that satisfies the gate |
|---|---|---|
| Information architecture | L3 | Organised, labelled content; navigation strategy; site map |
| Navigation flow | L3 | Persistent, sequential and hierarchical drill-down patterns documented |
| Task flows | L3 | Diagrams of the tasks users perform to reach their goals |

### Gate D - Make and specify

| Activity | Required from | Evidence that satisfies the gate |
|---|---|---|
| Wireframes | L3 | Low fidelity (paper) and high fidelity (no colour; flow first) |
| Clickable prototype | L3 | Linked screens covering the crucial scenarios, behaving like the real product |
| Visual design mockups | All | Full-scale static screens with colour, brand and graphics |
| Design templates and style guides | All | Working models that keep layouts, pages and screens consistent |
| Mood boards | L4 | Themes and moods from digital, abstract and physical references |

### Gate E - Evaluate

| Activity | Required from | Evidence that satisfies the gate |
|---|---|---|
| Heuristic or expert evaluation | L3 | Report against the nine heuristics in the parent skill, plus the four-stage affordance audit per primary action |
| Usability testing | L4 | Moderated in person, moderated remote, or unmoderated remote sessions with findings |
| Test cases and scenarios | L4 | Goal- and task-oriented scenarios derived from real use cases |
| Accessibility (ADA, Section 508, WCAG 2.2 AA or the currently agreed standard) | All | Verified by test, never implied by a checklist |

**Reading the tables by level.** Levels 1 and 2 require only visual mockups, templates and style
guides, and accessibility. Level 3 adds every Gate A-D activity except experience maps and mood
boards, plus heuristic evaluation. Level 4 adds experience maps, mood boards, usability testing, and
test cases and scenarios.

## 3. Launch gate (separate from the maturity gate)

| Outcome | Pass criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Useful | Persona-validated; tested against documented goals | Test findings mapped to persona goals |
| Easy to use | First-task success in a usability test without coaching | Task success record |
| Efficient | Task time benchmarked against a competitor or prior baseline | Timed task comparison |
| Pleasing | Rating of 4 out of 5 or better on a first-impression test | Rating summary and sample size |
| Accessible | ADA, Section 508, WCAG 2.2 AA (or current agreed standard) verified | Accessibility test report |

**Rules.** Any single "no" blocks launch. Passing four of five also disqualifies premium pricing.

## 4. Procedure when a gate fails

1. Name the missing activity or outcome and the gate it belongs to.
2. Either close the gap (produce the missing evidence) and re-run every dependent gate, or
3. Re-position the engagement honestly at a lower tier (standard or mid-tier).

## 5. Example (original)

A Nairobi insurer's claims portal is proposed as Level 4. The evidence pack shows Gates A-D complete
and a heuristic evaluation, but no experience maps and no usability testing. The team declares
Level 3, lists the two Level 4 gaps, and schedules five moderated remote sessions with claimants
before the launch gate is attempted.

## 6. Checks

- [ ] The declared level matches the lowest level the evidence fully supports.
- [ ] Every ticked activity links to dated evidence and an owner.
- [ ] Accessibility is evidenced by test at every level.
- [ ] All five launch outcomes have pass or block evidence; no outcome is averaged away.

---

Sources: Synechron (2018) enterprise UX guidance for financial and insurance products (five-level
model, activity-by-level matrix, five launch outcomes), which draws on The Design Ladder and Natalie
Hanson's UX maturity model.
